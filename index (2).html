c: Hello everyone. Today, our group will present a family conflict about curfews and lifestyle choices. Let’s start at school with Trâm and her best friend, b.

b: Hey Trâm! Are you ready for the biggest event of the semester? Maya’s sixteenth birthday party is this Saturday night! There will be a DJ, music, and lots of food.

Trâm: I know. Everyone is talking about it. I really want to go, b. But you know my parents. They are very strict.

b: Wait, don’t tell me you have to leave early again. The party does not even start until 10:00 PM! Everyone plans to stay until midnight or later.

Trâm: Exactly! My normal curfew is 9:00 PM. If I leave at 9:00, the party has not even begun. Last time, I left early and missed all the fun. On Monday, I did not understand my friends’ jokes. I felt left out.

b: That’s not fair! You are sixteen now, not a baby. Going out at weekends helps us relax after a stressful week of studying. You should talk to your parents and ask to stay until midnight.

Trâm: You’re right. I will talk to them tonight!

c: Later that evening, Trâm talks to her parents, Huyền and a, in the living room.

Trâm: Mom, Dad, do you have a minute? I need to ask you something about this Saturday.

Huyền: Sure, sweetie. What is it? Is it about your English project?

Trâm: Not exactly. It is Maya’s birthday party this Saturday night. Everyone in my class is invited. Can I stay there until midnight, please?

Huyền: Midnight? Absolutely not! Trâm, we have talked about this many times. Your weekend curfew is 9:00 PM, and that is already generous.

Trâm: But Mom! Why does it always have to be 9:00 PM? Midnight is normal nowadays. Times have changed! If I leave early, my friends may stop inviting me. My social life will be ruined!

a: Trâm, keep your voice down. Do not yell at your mother. Your mother is right. Midnight is not possible. You are only sixteen. The streets are not safe late at night, and we cannot protect you when we are asleep at home.

Trâm: You do not understand teenagers today! You do not trust me! You just want to control my life!

Huyền: It is not about control, Trâm. It is about your lifestyle recently. You have been out with your friends four times this week. You come home, go to your room, and go to sleep.

a: Your mother is right. Yesterday, your teacher called me. She said your grades are going down, and you always look tired in the morning. You missed two homework assignments this week. You need to balance your social life and your schoolwork.

Trâm: My grades are still fine! I only missed two small assignments because I was tired. Why can’t I have some fun? Studying is stressful, and spending time with my friends helps me relax.

Huyền: We are glad you have good friends, Trâm. We do not hate your friends, and we do not want to stop your fun. But staying out until midnight is not safe.

a: Okay, look. We love you, and we know this party is important to you. Arguing will not solve anything. How about a compromise?

Trâm: What kind of compromise?

a: This Saturday, you can stay at Maya’s house until 10:30 PM. That gives you an extra hour and a half.

Huyền: But there are two conditions. First, you must text me every hour to tell me you are safe. Second, you cannot take a motorbike or taxi late at night. Your dad will drive to Maya’s house and pick you up at exactly 10:30. Deal?

Trâm: 10:30 PM… It is not midnight, but it is better than 9:00 PM. At least I can see the cake cutting. Okay, deal. Thanks, Mom. Thanks, Dad.

c: As you can see from our performance, family conflicts are a natural part of growing up. Teenagers want more independence, while parents want to protect them. The best way to solve problems is to talk, listen, and find a solution together. Thank you for listening to our presentation!

Part IV — Useful & challenging vocabulary

New vocabulary from the listening transcript. This section appears only after you submit your answers.

curfew /ˈkɜː.fjuː/ (n.)

Nghĩa: giờ giới nghiêm; thời gian phải về nhà.

Example: My normal curfew is 9:00 PM.

stay out late (phrase)

Nghĩa: ở ngoài đến khuya.

Example: Could I stay out a little later?

completely left out (phrase)

Nghĩa: cảm thấy hoàn toàn bị gạt ra ngoài, không được tham gia cùng mọi người.

Example: I felt completely left out.

be concerned about (phrase)

Nghĩa: lo lắng, quan ngại về điều gì.

Example: We're concerned about your grades.

exhausted /ɪɡˈzɔː.stɪd/ (adj.)

Nghĩa: kiệt sức, vô cùng mệt mỏi.

Synonym: extremely tired.

assignment /əˈsaɪn.mənt/ (n.)

Nghĩa: bài tập hoặc nhiệm vụ được giao.

Collocation: miss / submit / complete an assignment.

compromise /ˈkɒm.prə.maɪz/ (n./v.)

Nghĩa: sự thỏa hiệp; đạt thỏa thuận mà mỗi bên nhượng bộ một phần.

Example: How about a compromise?

pick someone up (phrasal verb)

Nghĩa: đón ai bằng xe hoặc đến đón ai.

Example: Your dad will pick you up.

keep someone updated (phrase)

Nghĩa: thường xuyên cập nhật thông tin cho ai.

Example: I'll keep you updated.

slip (e.g. grades are slipping) (v.)

Nghĩa: giảm sút, sa sút dần.

Example: Her grades have been slipping.
